# UI and First-Run Design

## Reference principle

Classic EYEZMAZE GROW games keep the evolving world dominant, present the available choices as clear visual objects, and limit permanent controls to essentials such as tips and reset. Their rules are learned by choosing an item and watching how the world reacts. Campaign 1 follows that principle without copying the original presentation.

Reference: [EYEZMAZE GROW CUBE](https://www.eyezmaze.com/game/grow_cube/index.html)

## HUD hierarchy

1. **World:** always occupies the full viewport.
2. **Current decision:** three illustrated building tiles appear in a compact bottom dock only while a choice is required.
3. **Immediate state:** campaign, activity, move, civic level, and population use one slim top bar.
4. **Optional detail:** resources live behind the Stockpile button instead of permanently covering the city.
5. **Utility controls:** pause, speed, grid, help, and restart form one low-emphasis corner strip.

## First-run journey

### Welcome

“Choose. Watch. Adapt.” explains the full game in three verbs and establishes the eight-decision limit. It deliberately avoids explaining formulas or prescribing a correct opening.

### First decision

A coachmark points at the three building choices. Each tile exposes only the two facts needed to decide: what it produces and what it unlocks.

### First reaction

After Move 1, the second coachmark explains the repeated rhythm: Town Hall growth, new villagers, and all completed buildings activating. It also establishes the Move 8 threat.

The tutorial is stored as completed after the final coachmark, can be skipped immediately, and can always be replayed through “How to play.” Opening its modal pauses an active simulation.

## Interaction rules

- One high-emphasis decision area at a time.
- No permanent resource wall; Stockpile is inspectable on demand.
- Choice cards use building art as the primary affordance.
- Choice consequences are written as production and unlock statements, not lore.
- Speed is a single cycling button rather than a precision slider.
- All controls remain keyboard accessible and support reduced-motion preferences.
